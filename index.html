Common questions

How do I measure with a ring?
  1. Choose a ring that fits the finger you are measuring.
  2. Lay it flat on the circle in the Ring tab.
  3. Drag sideways, pinch, or use the ruler until the gold edge just meets the inside of the ring all the way round.

Look straight down from above; seen at an angle, the edge appears to shift.

I don't have a ring.

Use the Strip tab: wrap a strip of paper snugly around the base of your finger, mark where it overlaps, and lay it along the on-screen ruler. The Finger tab estimates your size from the width of your finger, which is quicker but less precise. You can also print a paper ring sizer from Settings › Printable ring sizer.

How accurate is it?

Ringsure draws the circle using the published pixel density of your iPhone or iPad model, checked against Apple's specifications to within 0.15%. That is a few hundredths of a millimetre on a ring. In practice, how precisely you line up the edge matters more, and a careful measurement is good to about a quarter of a US size.

How do I check it, or calibrate a device Ringsure doesn't know?

Tap Check under the size. Stand any bank card on the screen and line up its edge, or lay a common coin in the circle. Ringsure tells you whether the card matches your model's screen, and keeps your calibration if you save it. A device that is not in our table asks for this once before showing any size.

I'm between two sizes.

Jewelers almost always advise the larger one: a ring that is slightly loose is comfortable and can be sized down, one that will not pass the knuckle cannot be worn. Wide bands, from about 6 mm, usually need about half a size more. Sizing advice in Settings lists the sources.

Which sizing system should I use?

Ringsure shows the system of your region by default and all the others below it. Tap the system name to change it. The size chart covers US, UK and Australia, EU (ISO), Japan and Korea, mainland China, Hong Kong, India, Italy and Spain, and Russia. UK letters follow BS 6820; some brands run half a letter higher or lower.
